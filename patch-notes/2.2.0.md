New building blocks for mods: gear sets written as content, a way to look up the game's own recipes, a way for content to ask about a specific item, a way to tell which data on an item may safely go with it, sounds and particles that ride an entity, an effect on a stand-in performer, a loot table that says which of its payouts are the expected ones, a reward that pays into the roll it came from, a reward that hands over more of the item a moment is about, rolls a mod adds to a loot table (and outcomes to an enhancement pool) while it runs, a step that counts a whole family of places by their shared name, items whose use counts toward a quest, pays out or puts on a costume, shops and boards that come and go with a feature, the Asset Editor's own pick list for a field naming a game asset, a yearly calendar for events that come round every year, the Almanac, a page for each seasonal event, one menu for quests, achievements, the Almanac, records and each player's own settings, quests that read as a journal, achievements that open on an overview with a Statistics view beside it, finished quests that count on any server, yearly achievements and capstones that pick their own set, a step for an item a reward hands over, titles players earn and choose, conversation lines that come back each day, join other conversations or pay out, a costume as a reward, characters that come and go with an event, bonus finds on breaks, harvests and kills, adds that grow with the party, standing with a faction or a character on the game's own reputation system, board jobs only some players may take, one set of text sizes for every page, and a records screen for boss fights. Nothing built against 2.1.x needs a code change, unless it read or wrote a player's HUD choices directly or drew parts of the Objective Book itself (its theme, its side column, its tracked-quests panel or the page built by hand); the developer notes say what replaces them.

### New

- **A ready-made quest giver.** A content pack can stand up a quest-giving character without writing how it behaves: it names the library's quest-giver template and says only how the character looks, what they are called and what they hold. Pressing F opens their quests, or the conversation their placement gives them, and it works on a server with no other mod installed.
- **Gear sets.** A mod or a pack writes one file per set: the pieces that belong to it, and the tiers of bonuses it pays. A tier asks for a combination of things at once, such as two pieces on anywhere, four in the armor slots, or the full armor with the set's own weapon in hand (a spare copy of a piece you are already wearing does not count as the weapon), and every tier whose asking is met pays together. Each tier adds stats written exactly the way an item writes its own, and the full-armor tier can carry a look, a native effect that stays on while the armor does. Wearing the pieces is all a player has to do: the bonuses come on and go off with the gear, a notice says which tier just did, the set's look comes back after you die and respawn, and logging out in a full set and back in wearing nothing leaves nothing behind, even if the server switched that set off, or deleted its file, while you were away. A server owner switches any set off, or retunes one, from `mods/ziggfreedcommon/gear-sets.json`, and the content audit names a set that lists no pieces, a tier that asks for nothing, a piece or a stat or an effect that does not exist, and a bonus that could never do anything.
- **The game's recipes, ready to look up.** A mod asks which recipes a bench makes, which recipes use up an item (a piece of gear's own salvage recipe, say), which recipes make an item, and what an item's own crafting recipe is, all with the full amounts on both sides. It covers recipes that ship as their own files and the ones written inside an item alike, including any another mod adds, and it refreshes itself when recipes or items reload. A mod that builds on the game's recipes this way picks up a third party's gear automatically, with nothing extra written for it.
- **Content can ask about an item, not just about a player.** Conditions and formulas read the item a moment is about, such as a piece placed on a work surface, separately from whatever the player holds:
  - the quality that one item carries;
  - its item level;
  - how worn it is (an item that never wears counts as brand new);
  - how much it adds to a stat through its own armor, weapon and utility bonuses;
  - how many enhancement points it carries, in total or on one stat.

  Each one simply has no answer where there is no item, so a condition that needs one stays closed.
- **Which data on an item may go with it.** Mods keep their own data on items, such as enhancement points or a custom tooltip. A mod asks which of that data an item carries, and which of it no mod has said is safe to lose when the item is used up, so anything that breaks items down or melts them can refuse an item carrying someone else's data instead of quietly destroying it. Each mod says which of its own data may go, once, when it starts; the enhancement points and tooltip this library writes are already covered, and so is anything written by another mod's enhancement writer that names its data.

- **Sounds and particles that ride an entity.** A mod can attach a particle system to an entity, or to one named part of its model, and play a sound that follows an entity, and only the players who can see that entity receive either. A particle attached this way lives as long as its own asset says, so one that never ends on its own belongs at a fixed position instead, and a mod can ask whether a particle system ends on its own before it attaches one. A burst at a fixed position also takes a colour tint together with its playback cap.
- **An effect on a stand-in performer.** A mod puts a native effect on a spawned performer (the puppet it shows working in a player's place), an aura or a visual on its model, for as long as it wants, and takes it off when it says. A performer has no stat sheet, so an effect on it never expires by itself; the mod keeps the clock.
- **A loot table says which payouts are the expected ones.** A roll can be marked as the expected payout of its moment, a wage or a return rather than a find. It pays exactly the same; what changes is that a mod showing the results can present it as ordinary output and keep the windfall look for real finds. Every existing table reads as it always did.
- **A reward that pays into the roll it came from.** A mod can add a kind of reward that is only collected by the loot roll paying it out, say toward a total the mod works out once the roll is done, and register it once like any other reward. It reaches that roll's total from anywhere inside it, including a table the roll pulls in along the way. Anywhere else it pays nothing and says so in the log, and the content audit warns when a quest, an achievement, a shop offer or a board contract names one, since nothing there could ever collect it.
- **Rolls a mod adds to a loot table while it runs.** A mod that works out extra rolls for a table from its own content (rather than from a file that adds to the table) hands them to the library, and they join the table everywhere it is rolled: a chest, a quest reward, another table that pulls it in. They come after every file that adds to the table, never touch its pool, and never show in the content audit's view of the file, which still reports what its author wrote. Should the mod's code fail, the table still pays out without those rolls and the log says so once. An enhancement pool (the stat outcomes an item can be stamped with) takes added outcomes the same way: they come after the pool's own, every stamp drawing from the pool can land them, and the pool keeps the name and rarity it gives what it stamps.
- **Loot handed over one way.** A mod gives out a roll's items the same way every time: into the player's bag when it fits, and the rest in one pile on the ground, so the pile matches what the player was told they found. A fractional amount (one and a half items, say) always pays its whole part and has a matching chance of one more.
- **A reward that hands over more of the item a moment is about.** A loot table can give a player another of the very item a moment is about (one more of what they just picked up, say), copied exactly as the game handed it over, wear and all. Half a copy pays one about half the time, and the rewards in one roll add up before anything is decided. The loot check warns where such a reward is written somewhere it could never pay, a boss fight's loot included, when it asks for an amount that can never pay (zero, less, or not a number), and when it asks for more copies than one roll ever hands over.
- **Check every loot table and reward kind.** A server owner runs `/zigloot validate` to have every loot table and every reward kind file checked for mistakes that quietly pay nothing, such as a roll that can never happen, a reward nothing on the server pays out, or a reward kind with no command to run.
- **A step that counts a family of places.** A quest step or an achievement criterion that filters on a qualifier (a station, a difficulty, a variant) may say how that qualifier is compared: whole, as before and by default, or by its start or by a word inside it. One step can then count a station and its greater tier together by their shared name.
- **Items that count toward quests, pay out, and put on costumes.** Three new things an item can do when it is used: count toward a quest or achievement step, pay rewards written the same way a quest's are, or put a costume on the player it targets.
- **A step that counts using an item.** Using an item (throwing it, cracking it open) is a step of its own, kept apart from eating or drinking one, and the step can say how the item has to be used, so one counts only throws.
- **A costume you can always take off.** `/zigcostume off` takes off any costume you are wearing. A costume changes how you look and is never a debuff, so you can always end one, and none goes on over a transformation you cannot.
- **Shops, boards and wallets that come and go with a feature.** A shop, a bounty board, an offer, a contract or a wallet that asks for a mod's feature to be on (a seasonal event, for one) is simply not there while that feature is off: it is left out of every list, a contract is never posted and an offer is never for sale, and it all comes back the moment the feature does. A contract a player is already working on stays on the board's Mine tab so they can finish it, and a wallet out of sight keeps every coin, so nothing earned is lost.
- **Board jobs only some players may take.** One slot on a bounty board can ask for something of its own, a rank with a reputation, say. The board is drawn for the whole server, so everyone sees the job that slot posts, and a player who does not meet it sees the job locked, with the reason. `/zigcommerce validate` lists an optional slot that nothing on the server fills as a note, since the mod that fills it may not be installed; a slot the board needs is still an error.
- **Pick a game asset from a list.** A field that names one of the game's own assets, such as an item quality, can offer the Asset Editor's own pick list of the ones the server has loaded, so an author picks instead of typing. An enhancement pool's and a stamp's `Quality` offer it. The list is a convenience, never a rule: a file written by hand still loads whatever it names.
- **Pick an item from a list.** Where a storefront, a board, an offer, a wallet, a calendar event, an Almanac page or an Almanac tally names its icon, where a price names the item it takes, and where a wallet names the item it is counted in, the Asset Editor offers its own item picker. A hand-written id still loads, whether the picker lists it or not.
- **A yearly calendar.** A content pack can date an event that comes round every year, and the library runs it on its dates: content can show only while the event is on and vanish when it ends, a quest or achievement step can count taking part (once for each time it comes round, even across a restart), a banner greets each player once while the event is on (whether they were there at the start or join days later; when two events start for a player at once, their banners play one after the other) and everyone online as it ends, and the event can have its own creatures spawn in the world while it runs. A server owner switches every event, or any one of them, off in `mods/ziggfreedcommon/calendar.json`, and `/zigcalendar` lists the events, shows one in detail, starts or stops one by hand and reloads them.
- **The Almanac.** A list of the seasonal events the server runs, and a page for each. A season that is on comes first in the list. Under it, a year at a glance marks the months each season runs, and a card keeps your own record: how many seasons you have taken part in and how many keepsakes you hold.
  - A season's page opens on a banner with its name, whether it is on and how many days are left (or when it comes back), and the dates it runs every year.
  - A chip for each year lets you read any past season, or every season at once. Your numbers for that year show as tiles, each with your total across every season and the whole server's. The server's totals are counts only and name nobody.
  - A shelf holds the season's keepsake for each year, earned, still to earn or missed, and a bar shows how many of the season's achievements you hold.
  - The banner is whatever the season's pack gives it: a layout of the season's own items, or a picture. A server owner can override it per season in `mods/ziggfreedcommon/almanac.json` with a picture of their own, a choice between the picture and the built banner, or a layout of their own; anything left unset keeps the pack's, and a value that cannot be read keeps the pack's too, with a warning in the log. A season with neither shows its own icon.
  - Open it with `/zigalmanac open`. A server owner can turn it off in the same file. It opens inside the menu, so the other tabs are one click away.
- **One menu for quests, achievements, the Almanac and records.** The Objective Book and the new `/ziggui` command open a menu with tabs down the left: Quests, Achievements, the Almanac (while the server lists a season), Records (once a boss fight has a record), Reputation (once someone knows you) and Settings. Another mod can add its own tabs to the same list. It looks like the game's own menus, with a picture beside every tab, and every page in it is the same size.
- **Quests read as a journal.** The Quests tab lists your quests in sections, each with its count: Ready to collect, In progress and Available open, and Not yet, Waiting and Completed folded away. Pick one and its page opens beside the list, with its steps, its rewards and its buttons: Accept, Hand in, Collect, Abandon, and Track or Untrack. A quest you can only take from the character who gives it says who to talk to. A mark on the row shows a quest you track. The column of tracked quests is gone; the quest tracker on screen still shows them. Filter by status or category, or search.
- **A quest giver shows the same page.** Their quest screen keeps its two lists, theirs and yours, and opens a quest on the same page and buttons as the book, so it reads the same in both places. Its Comes back, Locked and Completed sections start folded, as in the journal.
- **Achievements open on an overview.** How many you have earned, your points and any rewards waiting to be collected, the next milestone where the server has them, a tile for each category with its progress (the book's, the Almanac's and the title screen's tiles all wear the game's own Memories card), and three short lists: what you pinned, what you earned lately and what you nearly have. A seasonal category's tile says On now while its event runs.
  - The milestone's bar marks each rung along the way. A category you are still working through wears a ring showing how far along you are, and its tile marks what you earned there since you last opened it.
  - A tile opens Browse on that category, grouped by subcategory. Filter by In progress, Earned, Waiting or Feats, sort, or search; a long section shows 40 rows, then a button for more.
  - Every achievement has a page: what it asks, the tiers of its ladder, its rewards and whether each is collected, and a few more from the same group. Pin it from there, and collect its rewards there.
  - A season's achievements sit under the season's own name, in your language.
  - Track on a quest and Pin on an achievement say what they do when you hover them.
- **A Statistics view.** Beside Overview and Browse, Statistics lists your lifetime numbers in sections that mods add, and a row opens its detail in place, so Back keeps your place. The Almanac adds Seasons: a row for each season with how many times you took part, opening to your tallies for it and a button to its Almanac page. Where nothing fills it, the view stays hidden.
- **Views you can tell from filters.** Overview, Browse and Statistics sit at the top of the Achievements page as tabs, each with its own picture, so they never look like the filters under them.
- **Categories and seasons a pack can dress.** A pack can give a quest category its own picture and name, and an achievement category a colour and the calendar event it belongs to. An Almanac page can carry its banner (a picture, or the season's items laid out where the pack places them), a colour of its own and links to other pages.
- **One set of text sizes for every page.** Every page, HUD panel and notice the library draws takes its text sizes from one shared list, and a mod built on the library can use the same list for its own pages, so one edit resizes them all. Text is bigger across the board: small print is larger, and row labels, names and buttons sit a step above it. Headings and titles keep their size. The short words at the ends of the progress bars stay small so they still fit.
- **Owners choose when the Almanac tab shows.** In `mods/ziggfreedcommon/almanac.json`, `"$MenuTab": { "Show": false }` hides it and `"OnlyWhileLive": true` shows it only while a season is running. By default it shows whenever the Almanac lists a season.
- **A Settings tab for your own choices.** The menu's last tab. Show or hide the quest tracker and pick where it sits; choose how many quest and achievement notices you see (Every update, Milestones, Finishes or None); show, hide or move the XP and reputation gains and the crafting progress; and open the title picker. Every choice is kept the moment you make it. `/zighud` on its own opens it.
- **Owners set the defaults, and can fix them.** `mods/ziggfreedcommon/player-settings.json` sets whether the quest tracker shows and how many notices a player gets before they choose. `Locked` fixes a choice for everyone, where the tracker sits included: the choice leaves the Settings tab, and each player's own choice comes back when you lift the lock. Each HUD panel file takes the same `Player` group for its own display, and `/zighud` refuses to change a choice the server has fixed.
- **The quest tracker can move.** It ships with two spots, right and left, and a spot file offers itself to the tracker by listing `Quest_Tracker` in its `Panels`.
- **Quest notices follow your level, and leave a tracked quest alone.** A quest showing on your quest tracker pops no corner notices for its steps, its finish or its reward, since the tracker already shows them; inside a menu they still appear. Any other quest notifies at your level. The default is Every update: every step shows, on one line that updates in place. Owners who want fewer notices set `Notifications.Level.Default` to `"Milestones"` in `mods/ziggfreedcommon/player-settings.json`.
- **The HUD settings page is the server's layout.** It opens from a tile on the Settings tab for the players a mod lets change the layout (with MMO Skill Tree, its admins); everyone's own HUD choices live on the Settings tab. The two displays are named for what they show: XP and reputation gains, and Crafting progress.
- **Finished quests count on any server.** Achievements and quest steps that count finished quests move whichever mods the server runs. A quest counts once, when its reward is collected, so one that holds its reward for you counts when you pick it up.
- **Yearly achievements.** A mod or pack can tie an achievement to a calendar event. The library keeps one copy for each year the event runs: a copy counts progress only while that year's event is on, and afterwards it stays as a trophy for whoever earned it. Its text and its rewards can name the year.
- **Capstones that pick their own set.** A capstone achievement can name a category, a subcategory or tags and needs every achievement that matches, instead of listing each one.
- **Feats and retired achievements, written in the file.** An achievement can mark itself a feat of strength, or name the version that retired it.
- **A step for receiving a reward.** Quests and achievements can count an item a reward hands over, from a quest, an achievement, a shop or a boss.
- **Named keepsakes.** A reward can give the item it hands over a name of its own, such as the year it was earned.
- **Titles.** A mod or a pack writes one small file per title, and a player earns one from a quest or an achievement, or from an administrator's command. Each player picks the one title they show, or none, on a new title screen (`/zigtitle open`). It shows the titles they have earned as tiles, and every title still to earn with the achievement that gives it and how far along they are; picking one opens that achievement in the book. The title a player shows appears beside their name on the leaderboard, results, party and queue screens, whether they are online or not, so a title never gives away who is playing. A server owner can switch a title off without taking it from anybody who earned it. Titles never show in chat or over a player's head.
- **Reputation.** A content pack can give players standing with a faction or with a single character, kept in the game's own reputation system. Every reputation climbs one ladder of seven ranks: Hated, Unfriendly, Neutral, Friendly, Honored, Revered and Exalted. A pack can give the ranks names of its own, cap how far a player can climb, or let it run on past Exalted and pay a reward each time a player climbs another step beyond it.
  - Standing comes from rewards (a quest, a board contract, a shop purchase, an achievement or a conversation line can pay it) and from putting down the creatures a reputation cares about. A daily word with a character is one more way, written as part of their conversation.
  - Gear can add to it while you wear it. What your gear adds counts for every lock, conversation line, offer and rank, and the Reputation tab shows it apart, as in "(+100 from gear)". The characters a reputation belongs to treat you by the standing you earned, gear aside.
  - Each gain moves a bar on the XP and reputation gains display, and a notice with a sound tells you when your rank goes up.
  - A Reputation tab joins the menu once someone knows you: each reputation with your rank, how far you are from the next one, and what your gear adds.
  - The game keeps your standing itself, so it survives death and logging out.
  - Quest steps and achievements can ask for a rank. A pack can name that rank on the lock, such as "Requires Friendly with the Traders"; otherwise it reads "Requires a rank with a reputation".
  - A server owner switches reputation off, or switches off or retunes any one reputation, in `mods/ziggfreedcommon/reputation.json`. A reputation switched off leaves the tab and pays nothing, and the standing players hold is kept for when it comes back.
- **A line that comes back each day or week.** A one-time line or opening beat in a conversation can say "Period": "Daily" or "Weekly", so a daily gift or greeting is offered again once the day (midnight UTC) or the week (Monday) turns over. A player's save keeps one mark for each such line, not one per day.
- **A line that joins other conversations.** A pack adds a line to conversations it did not write with one file under DialogueExtensions: which conversations get it (every one, unless the file names some or leaves some out), which screens (the ones a conversation opens on, unless it names screens or tags), and the line itself, gated like any other. A one-time or daily line added this way is spent everywhere at once, so a player cannot collect it again from the next character. Changing or removing the file while the server runs updates every conversation, and the content audit checks each file once.
- **A conversation line can pay out.** A conversation line can hand the player anything a quest reward can (an item, a roll of a loot table, an effect, a costume), each time it is chosen, or with a chance. Give the line a one-time or daily limit to pay it once, or once a day.
- **One greeting, several wordings.** A conversation's opening can come in several versions (one per rank, say) that share one daily limit, so whichever version a player hears spends it for the day. The opening can also pay out, once, when the player answers it or says goodbye; closing the window does not count.
- **A costume as a reward.** A reward can put a costume on the player who earns it, and the player takes it off with `/zigcostume off` like any other costume. It goes on a moment after the reward pays, so a quest, a conversation or an item can all hand one out. A player already in a costume, or in a form they cannot take off, keeps what they have; the reward can name a loot table to roll for them instead, and without one the server log names the reward that could not go on.
- **Characters that come and go with a calendar event.** A character a pack places only while a calendar event runs appears when the event starts and leaves when it ends, is stopped by command or is switched off, with no restart.
- **Bonus finds on breaks, harvests and kills.** A content pack can hand out something extra when a player breaks a block, harvests a plant by hand or kills a mob, one small file per find: which kind of moment, which blocks, items or mobs it covers (by name pattern, and the most specific pattern wins), how likely it is as a percent, and what it hands over, in the same loot format every other drop uses. A find lands where it happened: on top of the broken block, at the mob, or in your bag the way the harvest went, with anything that does not fit at your feet, and a harvest find can hand over another of the very stack you picked. A server owner retunes or switches off any find by name in `mods/ziggfreedcommon/bonus-rows.json`, and `/zigloot validate` checks them all.
- **A records screen for boss fights.** A server running the library alone can show the records its boss fights keep: `/zigleaderboard encounter` opens a fight's leaderboard (each player's best share of the fight, their total, the fastest clear, how many fights, and the damage they dealt and took), split by party size and difficulty the way the fight's file asks. Any player can open it; with more than one fight on record, name the one you want with `--encounter=<id>`. The menu's Records tab opens it too, with the other tabs beside it. The records live in `mods/ziggfreedcommon/` with the rest of CommonLib's files, and records kept by 2.1 move there by themselves the first time the server starts.
- **Adds that grow with the party.** A boss fight can give the monsters its spawners raise more health the more players are in the fight, the same way the boss's own health grows, from the fight's file (`Scale.Adds`). A fight that does not ask keeps its adds exactly as they were.

### Fixed

- **A boss fight's own payout can name a loot table.** A payout written inside a fight's script that named a shared loot table used to pay nothing; it now pays that table, the same way a fight's defeat loot does, and the content check names one that cannot be read.
- **A shop's own requirements are checked.** A shop that asked for something before a player could buy there used to sell to everybody; now each of its offers shows as locked, with the reason, until the player has it.
- **An offer switched off leaves the shop.** It used to stay on the page, locked.
- **A shop or board that belongs to one place stays there.** A shop or a bounty board that names the worlds it belongs to (a dungeon's own trader, say) used to be listed and opened everywhere. Now it is only there for a player standing in one of those worlds: anywhere else it is left out of their lists, a shop block or a conversation line that opens "the first shop" passes it by, and opening it by name finds it closed. One that names no world is everywhere, as before.
- **Look rays, ground finding and block sounds survive the next game update.** The coming game update removes the way the library read a single block, which would have let a blink pass through walls, left a dash and a spell aimed at the ground blind to them, set things placed on uneven ground floating or buried, and silenced block-state sounds (a shrine lighting up, say). They now read the world the way both the current game and its update do, so they keep working on a server that updates the game before its mods. They read only ground that is already loaded; a mod placing things where no player is near loads that ground first.
- **A reach-a-place step keeps its map icon.** A quest step or achievement that asks you to reach a place showed the game's treasure-map item as its icon; the coming game update removes that item, so the step now shows the game's map, the same picture.
- **An earned achievement stays in the book.** An achievement a player earned stays listed after a server takes it out of circulation.
- **Shared conversation lines reload everywhere.** Changing a shared group of conversation lines while the server runs now updates every conversation that uses it, not only the ones read afterwards.
- **`/zighud place` and `/zighud default` say what went wrong.** Naming a spot that does not exist, or one that was not made for that display, printed a raw text key instead of the line that explains it.
- **A boss that gets away is not a kill.** When a fight's script lost track of its boss while the boss was still alive (it ran out of the fight's reach, or left the world without dying), the fight used to count as won and pay out. Now nothing is paid or credited, the fight counts as lost, and it starts over.
- **A boss fight won just before the server stops is kept.** A defeat in the last few seconds before a stop could be missing from the fight's records; they are saved as the server stops.
- **A board says Abandon, like the quest book.** Its button read Give up, and its notice said the contract was given up.

### Unchanged

- **The held-tool readings.** Tool quality, item level and durability read exactly what they read in 2.1.x.
